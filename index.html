A point of VO2max is 44.7 meters of track

Muscle age got its audit: grip, gait, and lower-body power each put a number on the machinery. The cardio side has its own field number, and it costs a 12-minute run. The Cooper test: run as far as the clock allows on a measured track, then divide by the formula, (distance - 504.9) / 44.73. Six laps is 2,400 meters, and (2,400 - 504.9) / 44.73 works out to 42.4 ml/kg/min. The same division prices every distance on the track: 2,000 meters lands at 33.4, 2,800 at 51.3.

The denominator makes the test an exchange rate. Every 44.7 meters of extra distance buys exactly one more ml/kg/min. At the 2,400-in-12 pace, 200 meters a minute, a point costs 13.4 seconds. Five points costs 224 meters, which at the same pace is 67 seconds of running. The gap between the 2,000-meter runner and the 2,800-meter runner is 17.9 points, and it was built 44.7 meters at a time.

The dose view changes what the retest is for. One reading is a photo; the trend is the treatment record, so the protocol is the same track, same time of day, same warmup, eight weeks apart. Run it at lunch after a different warmup and the delta is the warmup. A 12-minute test every eight weeks spends 78 minutes a year on measurement, and every one of those minutes buys a number you can aim a training block at.
